# Promotions and Prizes Policy

*Last updated: 24 September 2026*

## 1. About This Policy

This Policy applies whenever a Client runs a competition, giveaway, prize draw, or other Promotion (defined in Section 2). It is part of our Terms of Use and sits alongside our Privacy Policy, Acceptable Use and Anti-Spam Policy ("AUP"), and Marketing Platform Policy. Words such as Client, Member, and Campaign have the same meaning as in the AUP.

## 2. What Counts as a Promotion

A **Promotion** is anything with an element of chance or skill involved in winning a prize.

**These are Promotions:**

- prize draws and giveaways
- competitions judged on merit, such as best photo or best name
- instant-win, scratch, or spin-to-win mechanics
- mystery or random rewards where different Members get different outcomes
- "follow us and win" offers, including on social media
- referral draws

**These are not Promotions:**

- a standard stamp card where every Member who collects enough stamps gets the same reward automatically
- bonus stamp days or double-stamp offers that apply to everyone
- a birthday or welcome reward that every eligible Member receives

If a Member's reward depends on luck or judging, it is a Promotion, and this Policy applies.

## 3. Who Is Responsible

The Client running a Promotion is the "promoter" under Australian and US law. Loyalties Marketing is not the promoter.

We provide the platform. The Platform does not currently include a tool that selects winners. We don't organise, judge, or guarantee compliance with the law of any state, territory, or country. You are solely responsible for:

- checking whether your Promotion needs a permit or authority, and getting one if it does
- writing compliant terms and conditions
- running the draw or judging fairly
- fulfilling the prize
- following every law that applies where your entrants live

If we add a draw or chance-based feature in future, we will update this Policy first.

## 4. No Paid Entry

You must never charge a fee to enter a Promotion. You must not sell tickets, sell extra entries, or ask entrants to pay anything beyond the normal price of a product or service. Paid entry turns a promotion into a lottery or gambling activity. That needs a licence, and it is not allowed on the Platform. Raffles and ticket sales are not allowed.

**Australia.** In a trade promotion, requiring a purchase at the normal price is generally allowed, but state or territory permit rules may apply (Section 11). The purchase price must not be inflated to fund the prize.

**United States.** You can't require a purchase to enter a promotion that is decided by chance. If entries are linked to purchases, including entries earned through stamps, you must also offer a free way to enter (Section 12).

## 5. Terms and Conditions for Every Promotion

Every Promotion needs its own written terms. Entrants must be able to read them before they enter. Put them in the terms section of the Campaign or link to a page that shows them. They must include:

- who the promoter is, with a business name and address
- start and end dates
- who can enter, including age and location limits. Entrants must be 18 or over
- how to enter, and any limit on entries
- a description of each prize, including how many there are and the market value
- how winners are chosen, and the draw date or the judging method and criteria
- how and when winners are notified and how winners are published
- how long winners have to claim, and what happens to an unclaimed prize
- how prizes are delivered and when
- any permit or authority number the law requires
- how entrants' personal information is used (Section 9)
- how disputes are resolved
- for US entrants: "no purchase necessary" and the free way to enter, where required, that odds depend on the number of entries, and "void where prohibited"

Where the dashboard gives you fields for this information, fill them in accurately and completely before the Promotion goes live. Where it doesn't, you are still responsible for publishing your own compliant terms wherever the Promotion appears.

Your terms and promotion wording must not mislead. Don't advertise a prize you don't intend to give, and don't use "win" or "free" in a way that hides conditions.

## 6. Running Promotions Through Campaigns and Your Program

- Promotion Campaigns follow the Marketing Platform Policy in full.
- Entrants don't have to opt in to marketing to enter. Any marketing opt-in must be a separate, optional choice.
- Don't collect entries in a way that adds people to your marketing list. The only way onto your list is a Member joining and consenting themselves (AUP Section 8).
- If you announce a Promotion to Members, only Members who confirmed marketing consent will receive the Campaign. That is expected.
- A stamp card reward that is fixed and identical for every eligible Member is not a Promotion. Don't describe a Promotion as "the reward".

## 7. Fair Conduct and Records

Winners must be chosen fairly. Use an impartial random draw or, for a skill contest, judge against criteria you published in advance.

- Don't rig or influence a draw.
- Exclude the promoter's owners, staff, and their immediate families, unless your terms say otherwise and the draw is independent.
- Keep records of the entries, how the draw or judging was carried out, and who won, in enough detail to satisfy an audit or a regulator. Keep them for as long as the law requires. In some states that can be up to five years.
- Produce those records if we or a regulator ask.

## 8. Prizes

You must:

- provide every prize as described, in full, and within 28 days of the draw unless your terms say otherwise
- not substitute a prize unless your terms allow it and the replacement is of equal or greater value
- follow your terms for redrawing unclaimed prizes
- check the winner's age and identity where the prize needs it, and where the law requires you to

You must not offer any of the following as a prize:

- firearms or weapons
- tobacco or vaping products
- cannabis products or prescription medicines
- illegal goods or services
- gambling credit or lottery tickets
- alcohol, unless you hold the licences your state requires and follow the liquor promotion rules that apply. Alcohol prizes may need their own permit

Prizes may create tax duties for winners or for you. Tax reporting can apply, particularly in the United States. Get advice.

## 9. Entrant Data and Privacy

Personal information collected for a Promotion belongs to your entrants. You must:

- use it only to run the Promotion, unless the entrant separately agrees to more
- tell entrants how you use it
- keep it secure and delete it when you no longer need it, subject to the record keeping in Section 7
- publish winners' names only where your terms say so or the law requires it. First name and state or suburb is usually enough
- keep entry data separate from marketing consent

## 10. Social Media Promotions

If your Promotion runs on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, or another social platform, follow that platform's promotion rules. State that the platform doesn't sponsor, endorse, or administer the Promotion. Don't require entrants to do anything that breaks the platform's terms. Your Promotion terms still apply in full.

## 11. Australia

Trade promotions with an element of chance are regulated separately in each state and territory. The rules depend on things such as the total prize pool, whether there is an instant-win element, and whether the promotion is advertised through media. They change from time to time.

Before you run a Promotion with a chance element:

- check the current rules with the regulator in **each** state and territory where entrants live. In Victoria, that is the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC)
- get a permit or authority if one is required, and put its number in your terms
- follow the Australian Consumer Law, which bans misleading conduct and requires you to provide prizes you advertise

If your Members are in more than one state, you must comply with the rules in each state, not just the one where your business is based. We don't track or publish state thresholds because they change. You are responsible for checking them.

## 12. United States

US law splits along a different line, chance versus skill, and adds a federal layer on top of state rules.

**No purchase necessary.** Under federal law, you can't require someone to pay or buy something to enter a promotion decided by chance. Doing so creates an illegal lottery.

**Sweepstakes (chance).** If winners are chosen by random draw and any entry is linked to a purchase, including stamps earned from a purchase, you must also offer a free Alternate Method of Entry (AMOE), such as a free online or mail-in form.

**Contests (skill).** If winners are chosen on merit, such as for an essay, a photo, or a design, you can generally require a fee or purchase, because removing chance removes the lottery problem. Your judging criteria must be genuine and defensible.

**Official rules.** US promotions normally need written official rules that cover the items in Section 5.

**State rules.** A number of states, including New York, Florida, and Rhode Island, add registration or bonding requirements once a prize pool reaches a set level. We don't track these. If you run a Promotion for US Members, get advice for the states you are targeting before you launch.

## 14. Reporting a Promotion or Prize Problem

Members, entrants, and anyone else can report a Promotion they believe is unlawful, unfair, misleading, or missing required terms, or a prize that was not provided. Use **loyalties.marketing/abuse** and choose "Misleading or unfair promotion" or "Prize not provided" from the report type dropdown. We follow up from support@loyalties.marketing. See Sections 14 and 15 of the AUP for what happens next.

You can also complain to the relevant state or territory regulator, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission or your consumer affairs regulator, or the US Federal Trade Commission.

If a Client repeatedly breaches this Policy, or fails to provide prizes, we may restrict campaign sending or suspend the account, and we may report the conduct to the relevant authorities.
